# Cover Letters That Don't Sound Copied

> Three short paragraphs: why this company, why you, and what you'll do next.

## When you need one
Many tech roles make the cover letter optional, but a short, specific one can lift a borderline CV, especially for attachments and graduate programmes.

## A simple structure
1. **Opening (2–3 sentences)**: the role, where you saw it, and one specific reason you want to work there (a product, a value, a recent project).
2. **Evidence (one paragraph)**: pick one or two experiences that match the job's top requirements. Say what you did and what happened.
3. **Close (2 sentences)**: restate your interest, mention your availability (for example the attachment period) and thank them.

## Example opening
*I'm applying for the Backend Engineering Attachment at Kijani Bank Digital. I use your mobile app every week, and the way it handles M-Pesa top-ups made me curious about the APIs behind it.*

## Tips
- Keep it under 300 words.
- Address a real person if the listing names one; otherwise "Dear Hiring Team".
- Don't repeat your CV line by line. Tell the story behind one item.
- Match the tone of the company: a start-up can be warmer, a bank more formal.
- Proofread the company name. Sending a letter addressed to the wrong company is the most common mistake.
